01 — Selected Work

The work behind a product that hasn't launched yet.

No metric badges on any of these. There is nothing live to measure yet — so what's here is the reasoning, the trade-offs, and the calls I'd defend in a room.

01
Trust & safety Community

Designing ratings under a power asymmetry

A scholar rating their PhD supervisor is rating the person who controls their degree. Reviews from graduated scholars publish immediately — the guide no longer holds anything over them. Reviews from current students stay hidden until 75% of that guide's cohort has submitted and at least four exist, so no individual is identifiable at release. It costs visible coverage at launch. I'd make the same trade again.

02
Activation Instrumentation

Instrumenting before there was anything to measure

I defined the activation metric and instrumented the event funnel before our first pilot, so the cohort produced readable data instead of anecdotes. 12 research groups went in; 7 completed onboarding unassisted. I traced the drop-off to the invite step and rebuilt that flow — a fix I should have pushed for two months earlier.

03
Discovery Scoping

Narrowing v1

34 interviews with PhD scholars, postdocs and supervisors across 9 universities. The synthesis narrowed v1 from four surfaces to two and cut roughly 40% of planned scope. I wrote and defended the recommendation; the PM and founders made the call.

The product decision

The no-setup constraint was the whole product decision. Every SQL course I studied lost people at installation, not at learning — so I cut the "set up a real environment" version entirely and put the database in the browser. XP, streaks and locked progression are retention mechanics, added because the other drop-off cliff is the middle third, not the start.

03 — A method of my own

The CALM method

I wrote my own five-step method for diagnosing a metric drop, then worked it through 14 real products. The "is it real?" step exists because a large share of metric crises turn out to be tracking bugs, not user behaviour.
